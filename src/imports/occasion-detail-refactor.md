Refactor the structure of the Occasion Detail page so that it focuses on the life moment itself and the people connected to it.

Do not redesign the visual style of the interface. Only adjust the structure and content organization of the Occasion Detail page.

An Occasion Detail page represents a meaningful life event or moment.

It should provide context and relevant information about that moment, but it should not function as a planning workspace.

Update the Occasion Detail page to contain the following sections.


People

Show the person or people connected to the occasion.

Examples:
Sarah (for a birthday)
Emma (for a surgery)
Alex and Jordan (for an anniversary)

Each person should be shown with their avatar and name and should link to their Person Detail page.


Date or Timing

Display the key date or timeframe of the occasion.

Examples:
March 18 — Sarah's Birthday
June 2 — Emma's Surgery
Every April 7 — Memorial Anniversary

Indicate whether the occasion is recurring or situational.


Relevant Preferences

If the occasion is connected to a person, display relevant information pulled from that person's shared About Me sections.

Examples:
Gift preferences
Celebration preferences
Food restrictions
Care preferences

These should appear as reference cards that help someone know how to celebrate or support the person appropriately.


Related Plans

Occasion pages may display any plans associated with the occasion.

Examples:
Birthday Dinner Plan
Group Trip Plan
Hospital Support Schedule

Plans should appear as separate cards that link to the Plan workspace.

Do not embed planning tools inside the Occasion page.


Notes or Context

Allow a small section for contextual notes about the occasion.

Examples:
"Sarah doesn't like surprise parties."
"Emma will need rides to physical therapy."
"Memorial gathering will be informal."


The Occasion Detail page should function as a contextual reference page for a life moment, not a planning tool.

Planning and coordination must remain inside the Plans section.
